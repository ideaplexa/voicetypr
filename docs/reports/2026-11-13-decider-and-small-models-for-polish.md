# Faster Polish: decision models and small rewrite models

**Research snapshot: 13 November 2026.** Read the recommendations and Sections 1–5 first; the model inventory is a reference. No Voicetypr clips or inference endpoints were benchmarked for this report. **[INFERENCE]** marks our proposed design or calculation. **[UNVERIFIED]** marks a missing measurement or unresolved fact, not evidence that a product cannot do something.

## The short answer

**[INFERENCE] Start with a faster rewrite endpoint, not another mandatory model call.** Then test a decision model as a *skip gate* or *meaning checker*. A decision model returns a choice or probability, not the cleaned transcript; it cannot replace generation when words actually need changing. [TypeSafe primitives](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction.md)

## 1. What decision models do—and what their numbers mean

Jev accepts a shared state plus typed questions. `Choice` returns a selected option and option probabilities; `Score` returns a rubric score and probabilities; `Noul` returns P(yes); Jev 1.14 also has `Box` for image locations. Questions are evaluated independently in parallel. The transcript could therefore be classified as `leave_alone`, `punctuation_only`, or `light_cleanup`, but Jev does not write the new sentence. [TypeSafe introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction.md)

**Do not confuse three benchmarks.** Artificial Analysis' Decision Index, the public Hugging Face Decision Index, and a vendor's replica of AA are different evaluations. AA's current **v2.1** includes document decisions, tool use, knowledge, reasoning and option-order consistency—not dictation cleanup. Its latency is **each evaluation's median, averaged with index weights**, not consumer p99 or a guaranteed network round trip. Scores below are from model pages captured for this report; the November 8 v2 launch article has slightly different values. [AA current methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/artificial-analysis-decision-index), [v2 launch](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/decision-index-v2)

### Can a 4B decision model run locally?

**Capacity: plausible. Drop-in speed and runtime support: not established for Decider Flash.** Its card requires **about 9 GiB of BF16 weights plus working memory**, Python and CUDA. The 27B needs **about 52 GiB plus working memory**. A 4-bit 4B weight array is **about 2 GB by arithmetic alone [INFERENCE]**; that excludes vision weights, quantization metadata, activations and runtime memory. It is not a measured RAM footprint. [Flash card](https://huggingface.co/perplexity-ai/pplx-decider-flash-v1.2), [27B card](https://huggingface.co/perplexity-ai/pplx-decider-v1.2)

Flash uses **noncausal full-attention layers and a separate per-marker decision readout**, not a normal chat `lm_head`. Generic Qwen GGUF/MLX/ONNX support does not prove correct Decider support. **[UNVERIFIED] No measured Mac/Windows consumer-device Flash-v1.2 latency, validated llama.cpp/MLX conversion, or ONNX package was found.** A suitably large NVIDIA PC can follow the CUDA path; its speed remains unmeasured here. [Checkpoint serving requirements](https://huggingface.co/perplexity-ai/pplx-decider-flash-v1.2)

Real local decision numbers exist for **Drex Max v1**, not Flash: the vendor reports an **M5 Pro, 48 GB**, affine Q4, **1,022 input tokens**, medians of three runs: **1.66 s Kapitsa / 2.49 s MLX** completed requests. It requires **24 GB or more** for affine Q4; Q8 GGUF needs **48 GB** and uses a forked llama.cpp. Local is not automatically faster. [Drex card, Speed and runtimes](https://huggingface.co/nace-ai/drex-max-v1)

A better-supported local gate candidate is **Liquid d1-2B: 1.92B**, with documented Transformers CPU/MPS/CUDA, llama.cpp and MLX packages. **[UNVERIFIED] No comparable short-dictation decision latency was found.** Its LFM licence has a commercial revenue threshold, including downstream businesses—important for a distributable desktop app. [Runtime docs](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/models/d1-2b), [licence terms](https://docs.liquid.ai/lfm/help/model-license.md)

|---|---|---|

These are vendor examples, not evidence of a dictation deployment. | Ask whether *any permitted edit is needed*. Skip only on high-confidence clean cases; ambiguous cases get normal cleanup. |
| **Skip based on known context** | Wispr's [Auto Cleanup docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4283510616-auto-cleanup-control-how-much-flow-edits-your-dictation-beta) say command-mode dictations and recognized AI apps **skip formatting by design**. | **Small, specialized generator** | [Baseten's Wispr case study](https://www.baseten.co/resources/customers/wispr-flow) says fine-tuned Llama does transcript cleanup on dedicated AWS deployments. Vendor/customer claim: pipeline **under 700 milliseconds p99**, with **100+ tokens under 250 milliseconds** required from the Llama stage. | Specialization and serving control are a more direct latency lever than generic routing. This is not an independently reproduced SLA or a disclosed Llama size. |
| **Style/template selection** | [Superwhisper](https://superwhisper.com/models) documents optional rewrite after STT, including destination-specific formatting; a [Windows/Ollama maker, June 23](https://www.reddit.com/r/ollama/comments/1udaxrl/using_a_local_ollama_model_to_clean_up_voice) describes app profiles and reports small local models over-editing or dropping words. | Prefer an explicit app profile; use a classifier only for unresolved cases. Terminal commands need preservation, not professional-email styling. |
| **Verifier after cheap LLM; cascade when doubtful** | TypeSafe's [SDE cascade cookbook and code](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/sde_cascade.md) uses Luna → Jev field checks → Sol only on failure. Its separate [answer verifier](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/verify_llm_outputs.md) reports **88.5%** baseline correctness; **73%** passed, **95.7%** of passed answers correct; review caught **84 of 115** wrong answers. Vendor experiment on source-grounded QA, not rewrite fidelity. | A checker may improve trust, but will still miss bad rewrites and reject good ones. Ask separately about added facts, changed intent, and answering instead of transcribing. |
| **Deterministic post-check** | [Evoglyph](https://evoglyph.com/docs/cleanup-model) ships a fine-tuned **LFM2-2.6B**, **about 1.45 GB** 4-bit base plus **about 23 MB** adapter, MLX in-process, with span-level deterministic reversion and raw fallback. It did improve steerability. | Test against the direct fast-model baseline. A router can pick good models and still lose overall. This is agent evidence, not dictation evidence. |

### What goes wrong in dictation specifically

- **Long prompts buy quality at the cost of time.** Onit's maker says **N=20** few-shot examples were better than **N=5**, but made LLM latency **1.5–3s**. Small models could hallucinate unrelated text or copy an example; it falls back to raw text. Its local **~800ms** combined result is a typical-sentence maker report on M-series Macs, not a common test set across machines. [HN, January 30](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46824361)
- **The model answers the dictated question.** A November 2025 MacWhisper thread contains actual prompts and outputs where cleanup offered to review a script instead of transcribing the request. One commenter said a more explicit prompt still failed with the Haiku version they used. This is anecdotal and **not evidence about Haiku 5.5**. [Reddit, November 26–29](https://www.reddit.com/r/MacWhisper/comments/1p6tv7w/dictating_a_questions_sometimes_has_the_ai_answer/)
- **The gate itself can be fooled.** TypeSafe documents literal readings, numerical/date weaknesses, option-order effects and adversarial state influencing answers. A schema-valid decision is not a correct decision. [Known failure modes](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness.md)
- **Published product speed is not comparable pipeline evidence.** Willow claims **~200ms** average-length dictations in its own September test, and describes app-context adaptation before transcription. It does not establish a separate decision-model stage. Superwhisper explicitly says it **does not benchmark rewrite models itself**. **[UNVERIFIED] No primary disclosure establishing a Jev/Decider cleanup gate at Aqua Voice, Typeless, Granola, Willow, Wispr or Superwhisper was found.** [Willow methodology and claims](https://willowvoice.com/blog/dictation-accuracy-zero-edit-rate-benchmark), [Superwhisper limits](https://superwhisper.com/models)

The strongest X release claim is Perplexity's **“up to 5x faster”** for its v1.2 joint requests. It is a vendor claim, not measured consumer-device or Polish speed. [October 16 thread](https://x.com/perplexitydevs/status/2111166029539758120)

## 3. Small/fast generators: first useful token versus finished text

For text pasted at a cursor, **finished text** is the useful latency. A quick first token does not finish the job. Reasoning models may also emit hidden thinking before answer text. [AA latency definition](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-3-1-flash-lite-preview/providers)

The best matched independent comparison found is Bruce Li's **October 20–22** public-API study, with open [harness/configs](https://github.com/zbruceli/mercury3), randomized order and one US-West client. Its short-length sweep had **30 timing prompts × 3 repeats per configuration**, grouped with longer-output arms in a **630-call** experiment. **The short Mercury `instant` output was about 93 tokens. It was code, not a 30–80-word rewrite, and retrying calls were excluded from latency statistics.** Therefore treat this as an endpoint shortlist, not a Polish ranking. [Methods and §5.4](https://zbruceli.org/blog/mercury-3-vs-the-fast-autoregressive-models/)

## 4. Does supplying the decision make generation faster?

**Direct evidence: [UNVERIFIED].** No controlled dictation study was found comparing the same generator with and without an upstream label such as “AI-agent prompt; punctuation only,” including total gate-plus-rewrite latency. Better task clarity is not proof that hidden reasoning or TTFT decreases.

There is evidence for the related, narrower claim that **explicit effort settings change latency**. Li measured Haiku coding medians **0.97 s thinking off versus 1.35 s medium**, Luna **1.28 s none versus 2.42 s medium**, and Mercury short TTFT **0.29 s instant versus 0.57 s low**. Those comparisons do not isolate a routing hint. [Independent study §§5.1, 5.4](https://zbruceli.org/blog/mercury-3-vs-the-fast-autoregressive-models/)

Anthropic says effort affects **all output tokens**, including thinking, and is a **behavioral signal, not a strict token budget**: low effort may still think. OpenAI advises fewer requests, short outputs and speculative parallel work; its broad heuristic is that halving prompt length may improve latency only **1–5%**, not the same thing as a guaranteed local-model prefill improvement. [Anthropic effort](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort), [OpenAI latency guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latency-optimization)

**[INFERENCE] Use the label to constrain edits, not to justify shortened content.** Polish must preserve the user's 30–80 words, not summarize to win a benchmark. Compare a direct concise instruction, an app-derived label, and a model-derived label while holding the generator, effort, examples and output constraints fixed. Predicted-output decoding is a different optimization: OpenAI documents accelerating edits when most output is known; it is not proof that every nano/Luna endpoint supports it. [Predicted-output discussion](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latency-optimization)

## 5. Recommended pipeline and the experiment that could disprove it

**[INFERENCE] Recommended default: app/profile rules → one fast rewrite → deterministic checks → paste once.** Keep the raw transcript available. Initially run semantic decision checks in shadow mode. Only put a gate/checker in the user's wait path after it improves the measured latency/quality trade-off.

**[INFERENCE] Illustrative sums:** Mercury alone **0.30 s**; Jev gate + Mercury **0.392 s**; Mercury + Mammoth checker **0.406 s**; Jev + Mercury + Mammoth **0.498 s**. These add **different workloads' summary statistics**. They are planning arithmetic, **not a measured median, p95, p99, or an SLA**, and exclude unmeasured local work. A serial Haiku-plus-Jev combination would be **1.022 s** under the same invalid-as-a-benchmark arithmetic, showing how little headroom that option has. [Generation inputs](https://zbruceli.org/blog/mercury-3-vs-the-fast-autoregressive-models/), [decision inputs](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/artificial-analysis-decision-index)

**[INFERENCE] Gate break-even:** if gate cost is `G`, generation cost is `L`, and safe skip fraction is `s`, mean gated latency is `G + (1-s)L`; it beats direct generation only if `s > G/L`. Using the reference numbers above, Jev before **0.30 s** Mercury needs **more than 30.7%** safe skips. These are derived figures, not an observed clean-transcript rate. A gate that never skips adds latency even if its label improves quality. At high skip rates the median may improve while non-skipped p95 gets worse.

### What we must measure on our own real clips

**[INFERENCE] Collect consented, representative clips and blind-review the output against intended meaning.** Include English and actual supported languages, accents, short replies, long corrections, code/terminal prompts, names, numbers, negation, dictated questions, filler words used literally, and already-clean transcripts. Compare identical STT outputs so Polish is isolated.

Record separately: hotkey release → final STT; request sent → first answer token; request sent → complete rewrite; gate/check time; paste complete. Report p50/p95/p99 by region, destination and device, with cold/warm models, cache hits, concurrency, failures and retries included. Count zero-edit acceptability, meaning changes, false skips, unnecessary edits, verifier misses/rejections and raw fallbacks. Do not substitute WER or Decision Index for rewrite fidelity. Superwhisper's admission that it lacks rewrite benchmarks is a useful warning. [Product benchmark limits](https://superwhisper.com/models)

**[INFERENCE] Required ablations:** direct fast model; app-derived hint only; decision-derived hint; skip gate; post-check; full cascade; local generator. Tune probability thresholds on held-out clips, not the same clips used to write the prompt. Test reordered labels and adversarial dictated instructions because the decision vendors explicitly document sensitivity. [TypeSafe caveats](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness.md)

### Privacy cost of every extra hop

| Route | Exposure and decision [INFERENCE unless linked] |
|---|---|
| Entirely local STT + gate + rewrite | No transcript leaves the device by this design. Local logging/history still needs a policy. Onit's [maker report](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46824361) and [Evoglyph architecture](https://evoglyph.com/docs/cleanup-model) demonstrate this approach. |
| Local STT → cloud generator | Text and any supplied app context leave the device even when audio does not. Send the minimum context; do not silently attach clipboard contents or screenshots. |
| Cloud gate → cloud generator | Gate sees **every** transcript, including those it decides to skip; generator sees the non-skipped set. Different vendors mean another processor and failure domain. TypeSafe says no request/response training, but **ZDR is for enterprise customers**, not an assumption for every account. [Data handling](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models.md) |
| Cloud checker after generation | Checker sees **both original and rewritten text**. A second decision call to the same processor is another transmission but not necessarily another company; a separate vendor expands exposure. Baseten's Mammoth card says **ZDR on by default**; verify contractual scope. [Card](https://huggingface.co/baselabs/mammoth-1) |
| OpenRouter / Vercel / Cloudflare gateway | A gateway can add another processor before inference. Do not equate gateway routing with local or same-region execution. OpenRouter Flash v1.2 explicitly says **not available through EU in-region routing**; Cloudflare's Clef endpoint explicitly lists **zero data retention**. [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/perplexity/pplx-decider-flash-v1.2), [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/clef/) |
| Gemini free versus paid | Google lists content used to improve products on **free**, **not on paid**. Never use a free-tier assumption for private production dictation. No training does not itself establish zero retention. [Google pricing/data-use table](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) |

## What I would try first

1. **[INFERENCE] Direct Mercury `instant` versus Cerebras gpt-oss `low`, with app-derived mode and local preservation checks.** This tests the strongest independent short-answer latency evidence without paying a router tax. Keep Haiku thinking-off as a quality reference.
