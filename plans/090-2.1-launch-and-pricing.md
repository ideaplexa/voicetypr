Founder direction, 14 November 2026: lead with the whole 2.1 story, not one
problem. Welcome everyone, including people who are not technical and people
still on older tools such as Dragon. Stand out by doing things for the user's
experience that others do not. Plan the 2.1 promotion and the coming pricing
changes.

Status: plan. Nothing here is built, published or priced unless a line says
so. Every figure about another product comes from
`docs/reports/2026-11-12-audit/competitors.md` (snapshot of 12 November) and
must be rechecked on the day it is used in public.

## 1. What we say

One sentence for everyone: **talk the way you talk, and the text you meant
appears where your cursor is. Private, on Mac and Windows, paid once.**

Three promises, in this order, each shown with a real recording and never
with a synthetic clip:

1. **You see your words as you speak**, and the text lands in the app you are
   in.
2. **It comes out clean**, in the right shape for that app, without changing
   what you meant and without answering your question.
3. **It is yours.** Local engines work offline, nothing you say is logged, and
   you pay once.

Draft list of what is new in 2.1, taken from the beta.9 build. Check each
line against `CHANGELOG.md` before it is published:

- Live words in the pill while you talk.
- Polish with styles (Clean, Writing, Notes, Message, Code) and per-app
  overrides.
- The redesigned app: Home, History, Insights, Recording, Transcription,
  Dictionary, Polish.
- Faster local recognition with Parakeet, and Nemotron for 64 languages.
- Soniox real-time recognition with your own key.
- Spoken punctuation in eight languages.
- A recording is kept when something fails, so a take is not lost.
- Polish through an AI tool you already pay for (Claude Code, Codex and
  others), with no API key.

What we must not claim to Dragon users in 2.1: hands-free control of the
computer or editing by voice ("select that", "scratch that"). Voice commands
are Phase 6 (2.3). In 2.1 the honest offer is a modern dictation replacement,
not a full Dragon replacement. Dragon Professional 16 was listed at $699,
Windows only, with purchasing paused, in the 12 November snapshot.

Work this audience needs before we promote to it:

- The Windows checks that block stable (087-S2, 087-S4) and a real-speech
  pass on Windows. Without them the Dragon pitch is not safe.
- First run with no jargon on the default path: no "API key", "CLI" or model
  names until the user goes looking.
- Help centre refresh and help videos (plan 089); the 15 articles were last
  checked in July.
- A way to bring their words: import a plain word list into the Dictionary.
  Whether Dragon can export its vocabulary in a usable form is not checked.

## 5. Pricing

### Today (live site, read from the `voicetypr-web` source)

One-time, lifetime: Pro $39 for 1 device, Plus $69 for 2, Max $99 for 4.
Trial of 3 days that starts at first launch.

### Already decided (master plan)

- Trial: 7 days, starts on a click. Built on two branches, not deployed.
- Core: one-time, with one year of updates; the app keeps working after.
- Plus: a subscription at $9 a month ($79 or $90 a year) with managed cloud
  credits, sync and mobile. It needs accounts and the credit proxy, which are
  Phase 5 (2.2 to 2.3).

### Problems to solve before any price changes

1. **The name "Plus" is taken.** It is the 2-device one-time tier today and
   the subscription tomorrow. Selling both under one name will confuse buyers
   and support.
2. **"Lifetime" was sold.** Moving to "one year of updates" must never reach
   back to people who already bought.
3. **Device limits come from the key's prefix** and a local table today, not
   from Polar's answer. New or renamed tiers touch that code. It is the money
   path, so tests come first.

### Recommended order

| Step | When | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | With 2.1 stable | Only the trial changes: 7 days, on a click. Prices and tiers stay. | One change at a time on the money path; the launch is not confused by a price story |
| 2 | With 2.1 stable | Publish one promise: everyone who bought before the change date keeps what they bought, including updates, for good. | Removes the fear that stops people buying before a change |
| 3 | At least 30 days after launch, announced on launch day | One-time tiers renamed by device count (for example Core for 1, 2 or 4 devices). New purchases get one year of updates. | Frees the name Plus; the announced date is honest urgency in place of a discount |
| 4 | When accounts and the credit proxy exist | Plus subscription opens. | Cannot be sold before it works |
| 5 | When mobile exists | Entry tier becomes 2 devices at $59 to $69. | Already decided |

Not recommended: a launch discount. A date after which new buyers get one
year of updates in place of lifetime does the same job without teaching
people to wait for sales.

How each change is made: new products in Polar for new terms, never an edit
of a product that has buyers; old keys keep validating unchanged; one real
test purchase and activation per tier on Mac and Windows before the page
changes; the refund page and the EULA updated the same day.

## 7. Founder decisions, 14 November (these override the sections above)

- **Launch date: 27 November 2026.**
- **Everything planned goes into 2.1**, not 2.2 or 2.3: voice commands, the
  managed plan, the new onboarding, industry vocabulary. The master plan's
  phase map still says otherwise and must be rewritten to match.
- **We sell convenience, not only dictation.** Two offers:
  - **Managed** (subscription): we choose the engines; the user never picks a
    provider. Metered by hours of speech, not by device, so devices are
    unlimited. About $8 to $9 a month, $69 to $79 a year.
  - **Local** (one-time, lifetime): runs on the user's machine with a
    recommended setup, own keys optional. Priced by device, cheaper per
    device as the count rises, no unlimited tier. The entry price may change.
  - Mixing the two is allowed.
- **Onboarding asks what the user does** (for example medical, legal,
  software) and sets up vocabulary and defaults for it. The answer also tells
  us who our users are.
- **Channels:** X, Reddit, LinkedIn. **Founder support** is a headline
  selling point.
- **Teams and businesses:** be ready to sell to them; the site may describe
  how data is processed.
- **Dragon and Windows users** are one audience among several, not the focus
  of this launch.

### Cost check for Managed (estimate, not measured)

- Recognition at Soniox's list price is about $0.12 per hour of speech.
- Polish with a small fast model at $0.10 in / $0.50 out per million tokens
  is roughly 1 to 3 cents per hour of speech; our prompt size is not measured.
- The founder's own set is 8.66 hours of speech in 13 days, about 20 hours a
  month for a heavy user: roughly $3 a month in provider cost.
- Open: Soniox's written permission to resell usage (master plan 5.8), and
  the payment provider's fee per charge.

### Risks raised with the founder on 14 November

1. Thirteen days for stable plus accounts-free managed billing, voice
   commands and a new onboarding is more than has been built in any earlier
   two-week stretch on this project; the eight stable blockers and the
   Windows pass are still open.
2. Managed is the money path. A shortcut that fits the date: sell it as a
   subscription licence key and let our server meter hours per key, with no
   accounts or email system. Not designed yet.
3. "HIPAA-compatible" cannot be stated without signed agreements with every
   provider that handles the audio or text. What is true today: with a local
   engine and Polish off, audio and text do not leave the device.
