What makes it feel right

One shape, one spring, from the first frame. Width, height and corners chase one target on a spring (response 0.34 s, bounce 0.22), so the island answers the key on the very first frame with a slight overshoot. A new state mid-motion just redirects it. There is no dip when it opens from the dot; a rejected press (no mic, trial ended) dips 4% instead, so a "no" feels different from a "yes".

One steady row. Listening, transcribing and polishing all hold about 236 px: the app icon on the left, the main thing in the middle, the right zone handing over from the timer to the frozen duration, then to Skip. The pasted card is the same row ("Pasted" · "8 words") for 2.4 s. Live words grow it to 300×64, with the words beyond the row.

The start card only when it says something new. A setting decides: Always, When something changes (default) or Never. "Something changed" means this app's Polish style, the mic, the engine or the key status differs from the last dictation, or it is one of your first 5. When it shows, it stays 0.8 s. Otherwise the dot opens straight into the row, and the ✦ badge still tells you whether Polish will run.

The AI badge. A 12 px sage disc with an SVG star on the app icon's corner, overlapping it by 4 px and kept clear of the wave. It turns amber with a "!" when the Polish key is invalid and raw text will be pasted, and it is gone when Polish is off. When the start card shows, the ✦ flies out of its style chip and lands as the badge.

Quick settings, with the record button on the dot. Rest the pointer on the dot for 450 ms, moving under 50 px/s, and it opens: four chips on the far row (Polish style, engine, mic and language, which bilingual users switch), and on the dot's row the record button grown from the dot exactly where it was, the hint beside it and the gear on the far side. Hover then click dictates; so does a click anywhere on the panel. A click in the first 120 ms after arriving is ignored, and the 20 px hover target extends away from the screen edge only, so a pointer heading for the Dock or the taskbar never opens it. A chip grows the island into its list with the ✓ row by the chip (as far as the anchored edge allows), and a row ignores clicks for 250 ms or until the pointer moves 4 px. The start card's style chip opens the same list, for this dictation only.

A voice-note wave. A bar is born at the right every 70 ms from the loudest moment of its interval, and the strip slides left at a steady pace. Only elapsed time is drawn: a new dictation starts empty, the oldest bars leave under a 24 px fade, the newest bar is the brightest, and quiet stretches flow in as dim dots. With Reduce motion it becomes a stationary meter whose bars change height in place.

Shared elements. The app icon stays on screen in every state. The wave converges into the progress arc, and the arc closes into the check (or a ring with "!", a clipboard, a mic-off or a dashed "nothing heard" ring), so your eye follows one thing. Problem glyphs sit on a tinted 20 px disc, so a problem reads within 200 ms.

One card, never two. A blocker that opens on the island (trial ended, no mic, copied, no speech) turns into its stack card in place: the card lifts off the island and springs into the stack while the island folds into the dot underneath. Newest in front, older ones peek behind (3 at most, then "+N"); timed items leave in arrival order and their clocks pause while the stack is tucked.

Nothing said is lost. "No speech heard" is not proof of silence: the clip is kept for 30 s with Transcribe anyway. A mic that drops keeps what it captured. A failed translation pastes the original; a Polish timeout pastes it unpolished with Polish now; a rejected cloud key keeps the recording with Retry with Parakeet or Discard. "Finishing…" answers a press while the last dictation completes.

Copy rules. "Retry with X" everywhere. × dismisses with nothing lost; Discard only when audio is deleted. "Choose mic", and "Mic" in the pill. One title per condition, the same on the island and its card. "Open System Settings" on macOS, "Open Privacy settings" on Windows. Short engine names, and generic mic suffixes stripped. Every outcome is announced once to screen readers.

Any wallpaper, any edge. The island and the cards always draw two hairlines, a dark one outside and a light one inside, so they read on light, dark and busy desktops. Everything grows away from the anchored edge: at the top of the screen the record row stays on the dot, lists and the stack open below, and live words sit under the row. On Windows the real app must place the pill inside monitor.work_area() of the foreground window's monitor, so it never sits on the taskbar.

A dot that's alive. At rest the 12 px dot's sage core breathes on a slow 3.2 s cycle (amber without a mic). The pill is a non-activating panel (NSPanel on macOS, a no-activate window on Windows), so clicking it never moves the caret out of your app.

Scenarios. Each spec scenario runs on the lab clock, so slow-mo applies. Open ?scenario=<id>&autoplay=1 to start one, or use window.__lab.seek(seconds) for a settled frame at any time.
